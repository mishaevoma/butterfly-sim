Free flight

Free flight lets the body move under its own forces while its pitch follows the measured history, as in Fei & Yang's simulations. The controller trims stroke amplitude for climb rate and stroke-plane tilt for speed.

Fully free frees the pitch as well. Without help the body tumbles within about one wingbeat, as the Kyoto group found for Parantica sita; the controller then also steers mean sweep, and holds only some species.

A gust lasts one wingbeat and moves the air at 0.8 m/s.

What this model sees

Sees: the wake rolling up behind each stroke, the two wings pulling on each other near the clap, unsteady circulation and added mass, and how body motion feeds back into the air.

Approximates: leading-edge vortex lift, through the Polhamus suction analogy; skin friction, as a fixed drag coefficient; wing flexibility, as prescribed twist.

Blind to: where the flow actually separates, viscous decay of the wake beyond a core-growth law, and anything at the scale of the wing scales.
